- Sequential ablation, sharper strawman (`alloc/sequential.py`, `mode="maximal"`): stage 1 gives every agent the **highest-utility** row its error headroom allows, with no sight of the frame budget; stage 2 claws time back by repeatedly applying the downgrade with the smallest utility loss per unit of time saved, then runs the same greedy fill. This is the opponent `mode="cheapest"` was not, because its error-side commitment is the one stage 2 has to live with. **It also produces the joint solver's exact assignment** - 108 feasible cells (seeds 0-5, N in {120, 300}, with and without a headroom mask, budget 0.15-0.9 of T(0)), 0 differing assignments, 0.0000% utility gap. The null survives unstructured random tables with dominated rows (m = 12 and 40, 317 further cells, 0 differences).
- What the ablation *did* localise: **the greedy fill, not the joint relaxation, is what makes the allocation exact.** Disable `fill` on all three solves and they separate immediately - cheapest-first collapses (up to 85% utility gap, it never spends the time budget at all) while maximal-first draws level with the joint Lagrangian or goes **slightly ahead of it** (up to 0.22% at small N, 0.08% at N=120), because the bisection stops at a bracket end strictly below the budget and leaves the integrality gap unspent. Re-enable the fill and all three collapse onto one assignment. So the joint Lagrangian's contribution is **speed** (O(log(1/tol) . n . m) against the two-stage O(n^2 . m)), and the exactness is the fill's. `tests/test_alloc.py` pins all of this (46 tests in that module, 98 in `tests/` under `.venv`).

- **LLM-driven agents: PARITY rationing a language model (2026-09-26).** `llm/station.py`, `llm/policy.py`, `llm/schedule.py`, `tests/test_llm_agents.py` (6), `bench/llm_agents.py`. The expensive behaviour tier is a local LLM (qwen2.5:7b via Ollama, ~0.69 s a call, so ~1.3 decisions a second); the cheap tiers are surrogates.
  - **Setting.** People in a station decide among six actions from persona, minutes to train, ticket, perceived queue length and zone (576 contexts); decisions interact through the queue. The model's EXACT action distribution comes from the log-probabilities of a one-letter answer (temperature 0, deterministic): verified live, 20 random contexts re-asked give identical distributions. All 576 are tabulated once as the measuring device for true drift; the scheduler never reads the table.
  - **Mechanism.** A per-agent ledger charges each surrogate decision's KL(surrogate || LLM) since the agent's last LLM decision (chain rule: the sum is the KL between the trajectory laws over that stretch); an LLM decision resets it. Requests are made ahead, from each agent's predicted next context, aimed to land just before the decision (the deferred-application result, applied to model latency); agents whose ledger cannot take their next surrogate decision are asked for first; the rest of the budget goes by salience x drift.
  - **The guarantee is deterministic, not statistical.** Seen contexts are charged the EXACT drift (a paid reply reveals the model's answer there; recomputed when the surrogate is refitted). Unseen contexts are charged E_MAX = log((1 + 6 * floor) / floor) = 9.21 nats, the largest drift one decision can have given the reference policy's probability floor. An estimated bound (largest drift seen in the (persona, zone) class, +50%) was tried first and under-charged unseen contexts by ~0.8 nats a decision (true drift 2.6x the cap on a synthetic policy); the worst-case charge cannot. So true drift <= charged <= cap for every agent at every moment, with the scheduler learning only from replies it paid for.
  - **Results** (7B, 30 simulated minutes, 2 seeds, every scheduled policy spending the same ~1.35 calls/s). At N = 300 / 1000 / 2000 (the model can serve 45 / 13 / 7% of decisions): worst agent / cap PARITY **0.94 / 0.95 / 0.99x** vs view-LOD 1.73 / 1.81 / 2.23x vs round-robin 1.17 / 2.37 / 1.82x; drift per agent-hour **2.3 / 2.9 / 3.1** vs 5.0 / 5.6 / 5.5 vs 4.3 / 5.7 / 5.3 (~2x less at equal spend); drift among agents the viewer can see **4.4 / 7.3 / 7.6** vs 8.2 / 10.7 / 10.4 vs 7.4 / 10.8 / 10.2 -- PARITY beats the view-first policy even on the thing that policy prioritises, because it pays where the surrogate would be most wrong. No agent ever waited for the model (0 waits at every N). Surrogate alone is not viable: at N = 1000 the crowd boards 172 trains to the reference's 416 and misses 20 to 437. All scheduled policies track the reference's crowd outcomes within ~5-10%; PARITY is not better than the others there.
  - **Found and fixed on the way**: replies arriving after the decision they were meant for were held for the NEXT decision (now each request records its decision number and late replies are discarded); slot reuse after boarding let a reply reach the next person (generation counter); a queue hovering at a bucket edge flipped the context back and forth (perceived-queue hysteresis of 2 people); stalled agents acted on replies for a situation they had left (now discarded and re-asked).
  - Model choice: qwen2.5:1.5b was tried first and is nearly degenerate (only ever "queue" or "platform", the first two options -- position bias -- and barely sensitive to time pressure; a distilled surrogate imitates it to 0.04 nats, so there is nothing to ration). 7B uses four actions contextually and is confident (mean entropy 0.10 nats), which makes surrogate drift large and rationing meaningful. - **Invariant 3 is now a stated theorem with a machine-checked proof** (`alloc/guarantee.py`, `tests/test_guarantee.py`, 12 tests). The bound is stated, proved by induction, shown to be tight, and its two assumptions are named: **(A1) admission dominance**, r(c,k) <= err(c) for every context k, and **(A2) non-empty mask**, a rate-0 configuration always exists. The inventive step is the asymmetry: admit at a rate that can be bounded *before* the frame, charge what actually happened *after* it, so the bound holds using only information available at decision time. The obvious alternative -- charging the mask what the ledger charges -- needs the agent's future context, which the allocator does not have. The corollary covers deferred application (`PORT_SPEC.md` section 4): reserving `L_max * e_admit` up front restores the theorem for any latency <= L_max. - **The dual-budget claim is alive, in an aggregate form, and the ablation now supports it instead of refuting it** (`alloc/dual.py`, `tests/test_dual.py`, `bench/aggregate.py`).
  - *The gap it closes.* A per-agent bound does not bound the crowd. If every agent sits just under its cap, every agent is individually within guarantee and the simulation as a whole is as wrong as it can be. Measured on plaza (`bench/aggregate.py`, 600 frames): `max_i D_i` is flat at 3.61 -> 3.96 against a 3.969 cap as N goes 100 -> 800, while **`sum_i D_i` grows 115 -> 1012, linearly in N**, sitting at a steady ~32% of the N*cap allowance. Nothing in PARITY bounds that.
  - *Why the sum is the right quantity.* Invariant 4 simulates the view-invariant core exactly for every agent at every tier, so agent states are conditionally independent given the core and the crowd's joint-state KL from the reference **is** the sum of the per-agent divergences. Any crowd-level statistic -- flow rate, egress time, a density field -- inherits its error from that sum, not from the worst individual.
  - *Why it revives the claim.* An additive quantity over agents is a knapsack resource, so it takes a real multiplier. `alloc/dual.py` solves `max sum s_i q(c_i)` subject to `sum time <= T`, `sum rate <= R`, and the per-agent cap as a hard mask (kept: a shared budget alone would let one agent absorb the whole allowance). Two shared budgets that are **anti-correlated** -- the time-cheapest rows are the divergence-most-expensive -- so a two-stage solve has to undo its own work.
  - *Measured.* Joint strictly beats sequential in **84%** of feasible cells on the phase-7 table (binary divergence, so the aggregate budget is a cardinality cap on concurrent surrogates; mean +0.071%, max +0.712%) and **92%** on the full 12-level table (mean +0.800%, max +7.738%). Across ~180 cells sequential wins once, by 0.0010%, which is the greedy fill's integrality gap rather than a structural reversal; the test tolerates 0.05% and fails above it.
  - *Correctness.* Verified against exhaustive enumeration on 6-agent / 8-row instances with both budgets binding: **exactly optimal in 35 of 37**, worst gap 0.084%, and both budgets satisfied on every returned assignment.
- Note on scale: the joint-vs-sequential utility gap is real and one-sided but modest (0.8% mean). The contribution is the **guarantee**, not the optimality margin -- bounding the crowd's joint-state divergence by a constant independent of N is a kind of contract no LOD system offers, and the per-agent cap becomes a fairness constraint inside it rather than the whole mechanism.

- **The error ledger delivers its discontinuity preferentially on camera, and that is structural** (`bench/anticipate.py`). Measured on plaza, 200 agents, 600 frames, orbit:
  - **83.6% of reconciliations happen while the agent is in view** (480 of 574). The cause is not bad luck, it is the mechanism: an agent enters the frustum -> its salience rises -> the allocator promotes it off the surrogate -> reconciliation fires. Promotion is causally locked to visibility, so the snap lands on screen by construction.
  - **The snap is visually significant.** Position jump at reconciliation: mean 0.138 m, p95 0.566 m, max 1.82 m, against an ordinary per-frame movement of 0.055 m mean / 0.071 m p95. That is 2.5x an ordinary step at the mean, **7.9x at p95 and 21x at the worst**, i.e. a human-scale agent displaced by more than a body width in a single frame. The latent state changes on 98.1% of reconciliations.
  - **The frame budget does not govern it.** Sweeping `budget_frac` 0.10 -> 0.85 moves the surrogate population 180 -> 30 agents but the on-camera fraction only moves **84.1% -> 76.7%**. Not a budget property.
  - **Anticipation does not fix it.** Two mechanisms tried. Zeroing the headroom of agents the core predicts will be visible does force early restoration, but the mask overrides the frame budget, so it simply overspends -- 1.92 ms against a 1.275 ms budget on 574 of 600 frames, which invalidates the apparent utility gain. Predictive salience (`PredictiveRun`: salience from where the camera *will* be, using the core's tier-independent future position) stays exactly on budget and moves the on-camera fraction only 83.6% -> 75.2%, non-monotonically in the lookahead. The surrogate population is pinned at 150.0 across every lookahead: the budget fixes *how many* agents are surrogates and anticipation can only change *which*.
  - This generalises past PARITY. **Any LOD system that allocates fidelity by visibility will upgrade agents at the moment they become visible**, so any state discontinuity in that upgrade is delivered on camera. MassLOD has the same property; it simply has no reconciliation step to make it visible.
  - The next mechanism is therefore not a scheduling one. Since the promotion instant cannot be moved and the budget does not help, the transition itself has to be made continuous -- a coupled reconciliation that preserves the reference marginal exactly while minimising observable displacement. `sim/surrogate.py` already makes the surrogate's stationary distribution equal the reference occupancy, so a maximal coupling should be able to retain the agent's current latent much of the time instead of redrawing it on 98.1% of promotions. **Not implemented or verified.**

- **Coupled reconciliation: the same law, with the snap removed** (`sim/reconcile.py::COUPLE`, `bench/coupled.py`, `tests/test_coupled.py`). - **Invariant 4 carries a second, independent guarantee that had never been stated: a bound on positional discontinuity** (`alloc/guarantee.py`, `bench/invariant4.py`, `tests/test_guarantee.py`). `Core.bind` is the overlooked half of invariant 4 -- it clamps the fine position of agents at **every** tier to within `BAND = 2 m` of core progress, so full-fidelity agents are constrained too, not only surrogates. - **Cross-discipline reading of the architecture, and the mechanism it produced** (`bench/fairness.py`, corollary in `alloc/guarantee.py`). Read across fields, the error ledger is not an information-theoretic device that happens to resemble a token bucket -- it **is** a token bucket (accumulate, cap, drain on service), which is network QoS. QoS theory is explicit that a bucket supplies a *deadline* and never a *rate guarantee*: a flow served exactly at its deadline, forever, receives an arbitrarily small share. That is the gap, and it is measurable here.
  - **Free corollary nobody had stated**: an agent cannot stay on the surrogate longer than `cap / min_k e_rate[k]`. Measured on plaza over 1800 frames, the longest unbroken run is **379 frames for the median agent, the p95 agent and the worst agent alike** -- everyone hits the same ceiling, which is the ledger biting at `cap / e_rate[ctx=1] ~ 384`.
  - **The gap**: the ledger bounds how *long* an agent stays degraded, not what *share* of the time it is. An agent restored for one frame and demoted the next has a duty cycle near zero. Measured: **15 of 200 agents on the surrogate more than 99% of the time**, median agent 77.6%.
  - **The fourth discipline closes it.** OS schedulers solve precisely this with **priority aging**, so effective salience is scaled by time-since-full-fidelity: `s_eff = s * (1 + alpha * age / age_scale)`. At alpha = 4, over 1800 frames: agents above 99% degraded **15 -> 0**, worst continuous degradation **6.3 s -> 2.7 s**, utility cost **0.07%** (41.77 -> 41.74, scored against *true* salience so aging can only ever lose), **0 of 1800 frames over budget**, ledger bound untouched. The p50 surrogate fraction barely moves (0.776 -> 0.772): aging does not change the aggregate allocation, it redistributes at the tail, which is what a fairness mechanism should do.
  - **The design rule this produced, which generalises past fairness.** Aging enters the **objective**, never the mask. The mask overrides the frame budget by construction -- that is exactly what makes invariant 3 a safety property -- so anything placed there silently overspends. `bench/anticipate.py` put 574 of 600 frames over budget doing precisely that, and its apparent utility gain was an artefact of the overspend. **Hard constraints are for safety properties only; every preference belongs in the objective, where the allocator prices it against the budget and the cost is visible.** That rule is what made aging cost 0.07% instead of blowing the budget.

Aging adds an empirical starvation-freedom on top; it is not proved.

Measured here as 1 - NMSE against the phase 2 **ground truth** (`latent/train.get_data()[1]`, the 19-dim head targets, `head_err`/`head_var` normalisation). Scoring it against `decode(d, 16)` instead -- the first attempt -- gives tick-3 a quality of 0.9751, *above* full latent16's 0.8680, which is impossible: two error sources measured against two different references is exactly the incommensurability invariant 2 exists to forbid. **Validation: tick-1 has no staleness and must reproduce latent-16's recorded quality; measured 0.8515 vs recorded 0.8680, 1.9% apart** (mine averages over the corpus subset, the recorded figure is held-out only).

    | config | quality | us/agent | drift/frame | frames to cap |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | full latent16 | 0.8680 | 10.930 | 0 | never |
    | latent8 | 0.7870 | 10.943 | 0 | never |
    | latent4 | 0.5510 | 10.527 | 0 | never |
    | surrogate | 0.0000 | 4.854 | 0.01323 | 300 |
    | **tick-3** | 0.8319 | 6.310 | 0.01437 | 276 |
    | **tick-10** | 0.7655 | 4.693 | 0.15174 | **26** |

  - On **steady-state cost alone**, tick-10 is cheaper than the surrogate (4.693 vs 4.854 us) and far better (quality 0.766 vs 0.000), and the allocator never selects the surrogate at any budget. **That conclusion was wrong, and the reason it was wrong matters: the cost model never charged reconciliation.** Invariant 1 says tier costs are measured; the cost of a *transition* between tiers had simply never been measured at all.
  - **`sim/reconcile.py::promote` costs 39.3 us per agent** (110 us for a single agent, 23.9 us with coupled reconciliation) -- **four to ten times the entire per-frame behaviour step of 10.93 us.** A row with drift `e` forces a restoration every `cap/e` frames, so its true per-frame cost carries an amortised `R * e / cap`:

    | config | steady us | + reconcile | true us | drift/frame |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | surrogate | 4.854 | 0.133 | **4.987** | 0.01323 |
    | tick-10 | 4.693 | 1.529 | **6.222** | 0.15174 |

  - **The surrogate survives, and for a sharper reason than it previously had.** Break-even is 4.61 us/agent; measured is 24-110, so it is not close -- reconciliation would have to get 5x cheaper than the coupled implementation to flip it. With the charge applied the allocator picks `surrogate=328` at the tightest budget and abandons tick-10 entirely above budget 0.40. The surrogate's value is not its cost per frame, it is that **it minimises churn**, and churn is the dominant hidden cost. That is a much stronger justification than "one of four tiers".
  - Charging reconciliation is also self-reinforcing: forced restorations at the tightest budget fall from **15.21 to 8.31 per frame**, which directly reduces the on-camera snap rate.
  - **Coupled reconciliation turns out to have a second benefit** nobody was looking for: skipping the per-agent `rng.choice` over 1000 corpus points on ~60% of promotions cuts reconciliation cost **39.26 -> 23.91 us/agent (-39%)**, on top of the 87% snap reduction.
  - **The cost it imposes: reconciliation churn.** tick-10 hits the cap in 26 frames, so the forced-restoration rate rises from today's measured **0.29 per agent per second to 1.52** at a tight budget -- a 5x increase, and 83.6% of reconciliations land on camera. This is viable **only because** coupled reconciliation now puts the p95 snap at 0.072 m, below an ordinary frame of walking. Without that work this addition would be a visible disaster, and the two changes have to ship together.
  - **Caveats.** The tick-k cost is modelled as `CORE_US + (FULL_US - CORE_US)/k`, i.e. perfect amortisation -- real tick-k carries per-frame overhead, so measured cost will be higher and the advantage smaller. This is the behaviour axis in isolation; the four-axis interaction is untested. And the surrogate retains one property tick-k lacks: an 11x lower drift rate, which is what buys long uninterrupted degradation.

- **Reconciliation optimised 2.4x, and doing so flipped the surrogate-vs-frame-skipping result back.** `sim/reconcile.py::promote` 39.3 -> 16.4 us/agent, from two fixes: a tabulated inverse-CDF for the latent draw (`Surrogate.lift_tables`, 64 precomputed distributions, **21.0 -> 1.83 us, 11.5x**) and a lazily-batched slot search (**16.5 -> ~14 us**; the cost was `a.pos[others]` reallocating a masked array per candidate, not arithmetic). A first attempt that evaluated all slots eagerly made it *worse* -- the loop nearly always succeeds on the first slot, so eager evaluation does 7x the work. 33 tests pass, including the coupled-reconciliation occupancy check, so the law is unchanged.
- **The surrogate-vs-tick-k comparison is not robust; it turns on a constant factor.** Selection at a tight budget as reconciliation cost R varies (600 agents):

  | R us/agent | surrogate | tick-10 | tick-3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | 0 | 377 | 220 |
  | **16.4 (measured today)** | **1** | **489** | 110 |
  | 25 | 99 | 496 | 5 |
  | **39.3 (before optimisation)** | **320** | 208 | 72 |
  | 60 | 361 | 0 | 239 |

  The crossover sits at **R ~ 22-25 us**, and a single 2.4x optimisation moved the system across it. Both earlier conclusions -- "the surrogate is never selected" and "the surrogate survives" -- were correct for their own implementation and neither generalises. **This cannot be settled in the Python prototype.** In Burst-compiled C# reconciliation will plausibly be 1-3 us, which puts the engine firmly on the frame-skipping side.
- **A modelling error in my own analysis, against the surrogate's favour.** The same R was charged to every drifting row. That is wrong: surrogate -> live needs full reconstruction (draw d from pi_ref, lateral slot, gait phase), while tick-10 -> live merely resumes an agent that already holds a valid but stale `d`. tick-k's true reconciliation cost is far below the surrogate's, which pushes the crossover further against the surrogate than the table above shows. Measuring the two transition costs separately is the correct next step and is not done.
- Net effect on the cost model: `R * e / cap` belongs in it, R is per-transition-type not global, and the whole question is an engine measurement rather than a prototype one.

- **Reconciliation is charged per transition type, and that settles the surrogate question structurally rather than by a constant.** Charging one global `R` to every drifting row was wrong: surrogate -> live *reconstructs* state (draw `d ~ pi_ref(. | R, ctx)`, free lateral slot, gait phase, position snapped to the core point), whereas **tick-k -> tick-1 merely RESUMES** -- a tick-k agent never left the live state, its latent is valid and only lagged, and its position was integrated from its own decoded velocity inside the core band. There is nothing to reconstruct and nothing to snap. With reconciliation charged only where it is incurred, the allocator selects `tick-10=377, tick-3=220, surrogate=0` **at every value of R from 0 to 200 us**: tick-10 is both cheaper (4.693 vs 4.854 us) and higher quality (0.766 vs 0.000), so the surrogate is strictly dominated on both axes and the earlier crossover disappears. Frame-skipping avoids reconciliation *by construction*, so no optimisation of `promote()` can close that gap.
  - **The two caveats that keep this from being final.** (1) tick-k cost is modelled as perfect amortisation `CORE + (FULL - CORE)/k`; real tick-k carries per-frame overhead and this is the single critical unmeasured number -- if tick-10's true cost exceeds 4.854 us the surrogate is cheaper again. (2) The surrogate scores quality **0.000** because `AXIS_QUALITY[0][3] = 1 - 1.0`, i.e. "no better than predicting the mean". But the surrogate's entire design -- the Metropolis-Hastings correction making its stationary distribution match the reference occupancy exactly -- targets *aggregate* correctness, which a per-agent trajectory NMSE cannot see. **The surrogate may be dominated by a metric that cannot measure what it was built to preserve.** Scoring it on an aggregate statistic is the honest test and it has not been done.
- **The mechanisms hold together, now tested jointly** (`tests/test_ensemble.py`, 7 tests). Coupled reconciliation, priority aging and the band ablation each change something the others depend on, and every bound had only ever been verified in isolation. All four combinations of {coupling, aging} satisfy the ledger cap, the core band, the demotion snap bound, bounded continuous degradation and the frame budget simultaneously; aging still removes starvation with coupling on, and coupling still shrinks the snap with aging on.
- **A regression guard on invariant 1's *completeness*** (`bench/costaudit.py`, `tests/test_costaudit.py`). The reconciliation omission was invisible because nothing checked that the cost model accounts for every cost -- only that the costs it knows about are measured. The general form is a model whose residual correlates with an observable it does not track, so the audit regresses measured crowd time on the model's prediction and correlates the residual against ignored counts. Result: slope **2.106** (the model predicts less than half the real crowd cost) and residual correlation **0.538** with the reconciliation count. The first version of this audit timed only `_fine_core` and reported a clean bill -- `promote()` runs inside `_allocate`, so it instrumented a region that excludes the very cost it was looking for. `test_cost_model_is_complete` is a **strict xfail** that becomes an XPASS the moment the `R * e / cap` term lands.

- **Hull allocator: the per-frame work is a sort, not a table scan** (`alloc/hull.py`, `tests/test_hull.py`, 9 tests). Every agent chooses from the same menu at the same prices, so `argmax_j [s_i q_j - lam c_j] = argmax_j [q_j - (lam/s_i) c_j]` and the choice turns on one scalar `theta_i = lam/s_i`. As theta grows the argmax walks the **upper concave hull** of the (cost, quality) points, so sorting agents by salience makes the assignment a step function: block boundaries by one binary search per hull edge, total cost by prefix sums, **O(K log n) per lambda instead of O(n m)**. The pruned 180-row table has **8 hull vertices**; everything else is chosen by no agent at any lambda.

  | N | serial ms | hull ms | speedup |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 200 | 0.92 | 0.67 | 1.4x |
  | 1000 | 6.68 | 0.70 | 9.6x |
  | 2000 | 20.1 | 0.76 | **26.7x** |
  | 10000 | 347 | 1.84 | **188.7x** |

  Utility within **0.005%** of the Lagrangian-plus-fill (worst case over the test grid 0.26%, which is the integrality gap: the hull is the exact LP optimum, the greedy fill rounds slightly better), budget and error mask respected everywhere. Prior art is Sinha & Zoltners 1979 / Dudzinski & Walukiewicz 1987 for the hull solution of MCKP and Funkhouser & Sequin 1993 for LOD-as-knapsack; what is specific here is that a *shared* menu collapses the search to a sort by salience. **Caveat, load-bearing:** this requires quality to be agent-independent. It is today; phase 6's per-agent INT8 scoring would break it and the fallback is `alloc/serial.py`.
  - A first version grouped agents by **headroom value** rather than by **feasible set**, which with continuous headroom builds one hull and one sort per agent and made the allocator **25x slower** than the one it replaces. The error column has two levels, so there are at most two groups however many headroom values appear. `test_groups_by_feasible_set_not_by_headroom_value` pins it.
- **A neutral metric both policies can be scored on: Crowd Fidelity Deviation** (`bench/cfd.py`, `tests/test_cfd.py`, 6 tests). Every comparison in this repo until now was open to a fair objection -- utility is PARITY's *objective* and divergence is computed from PARITY's *own surrogate model*, so MassLOD is being graded on someone else's exam. CFD scores both against the `reference` condition (full fidelity, no LOD), which is what both approximate, using only positions and velocities. It is **distributional**, because per-trajectory error is useless in a chaotic simulation, and it is reported **as a multiple of a noise floor** obtained by running the reference against itself with different behaviour randomness -- so 1.00 means indistinguishable at this sample size.

  | observable | floor | MassLOD | PARITY | MassLOD/floor | PARITY/floor |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | density | 0.1094 | 0.2262 | 0.1379 | 2.07 | **1.26** |
  | speed | 0.0915 | 0.1439 | 0.2085 | **1.57** | 2.28 |
  | spacing | 0.1892 | 0.1964 | 0.1956 | 1.04 | 1.03 |
  | ALL | 0.1300 | 0.1888 | 0.1807 | 1.45 | **1.39** |

  PARITY wins overall but the result is **nuanced, not a sweep**: much better on spatial density, **worse on the speed distribution**, and both at the noise floor on spacing. This is the first comparison in the project that MassLOD's authors would accept.
- **The metric found a defect on its first run.** `sim/tiered.py:341` gives surrogate agents `core.mbar[ctx]` -- a per-context **mean** speed multiplier -- instead of their decoded `speed_scale(dec)`, so every surrogate agent in a context moves at the same multiple of its nominal speed. Measured speed std: reference 0.2931, baseline 0.2767, PARITY 0.2308, and within PARITY, surrogate agents **0.2082** against live agents 0.2670. **The Metropolis-Hastings correction makes region occupancy exact but leaves the speed marginal unconstrained**, so it collapses toward a conditional mean. That is exactly the kind of thing a per-agent quality NMSE cannot see and an aggregate metric can. Fix is to sample a speed scale consistent with the region's reference distribution rather than average it; not applied, because it changes the core simulation.

- **Surrogate speed marginal fixed** (`sim/tiered.py::SURROGATE_SPEED`, on by default; `False` reproduces the recorded sweep). Surrogate agents used `core.mbar[ctx]`, the per-context MEAN speed scale, for both their reported velocity AND their displacement (their position rode the core point, which `core.step` advances at `speed * mbar`) -- so patching only the velocity would have fixed the CFD reading while the agents still moved at the mean. Now each surrogate agent draws a speed scale from the reference law for its current region, `speed_scale(decode(d))` with `d ~ pi_ref(. | R, ctx)` via `Surrogate.lift_tables`, redrawn when its region changes, and carries an along-route progress offset from its core point that integrates the difference from the mean. The drawn position goes through `Core.bind` exactly as live agents' do, so invariant 4 holds by construction rather than by arithmetic on the progress parameter.
  - **Speed std 0.2308 -> 0.2905** against a reference of 0.2931 (surrogate agents 0.2039 -> 0.2847). **CFD speed 2.28 -> 1.38 floors**, now better than MassLOD's 1.57; density unchanged at 1.27; spacing 1.00; **overall 1.39 -> 1.17** against MassLOD's 1.45. Ledger within cap, band within bound.
  - **Demotion no longer teleports**: carrying the live agent's along-route position into the surrogate takes the uncoupled demotion snap max from **2.06 m to 0.074 m**. Promotion p95 with coupling stays at 0.071 m.
  - Three bugs in the first version, all caught by the suite: clamping the *progress parameter* to +-BAND does not bound `|project(pos) - s|` near a corner once a lateral offset is added (band reached 3.49 m in corridor) -- fixed by binding the drawn position; `promote()` still restored agents to `core.point(s)`, reintroducing an along-route snap of up to BAND on every promotion and undoing coupling -- fixed with an `s_at` argument; and the offset was never reset when an agent completed its route. One test of mine was also wrong: it compared demotion snaps to judge coupling, which only acts on promotion.
  - **Changes the core simulation, so it joins the batch that invalidates the recorded sweep** (corrected ledger, `bind` corner residual, reconciliation charge, aggregate budget).

## DECISIONS
- Repo root is `cgvr/`; `sim/`, `bench/`, `tests/` sit at root.
- Tier index: 0=HIGH, 1=MED, 2=LOW, 3=OFF.
- One significance scalar per agent: 3D distance to camera, multiplied by 2.0 when out of view (after visibility hysteresis). Visual and sim tiers each apply their own band table, hysteresis state, and per-level caps to that scalar. Visual is forced OFF when out of view; sim is not.
- Distance hysteresis is 10% on band edges. Visibility hysteresis is a 15-frame hold after leaving the frustum. Caps demote lowest-significance agents first; hysteresis state stores the pre-cap tier.
- Tiers are recorded only. Motion is full-rate reference dynamics (goal-seek plus O(N^2) chunked separation, no ORCA) for every agent regardless of tier.
- Log holds integer histograms only (no floats), so byte-identity is exact. Fixed dt 1/30, single `default_rng(seed)`.
- Phase 2 heads: behaviour head = preferred-velocity modifier (3) + gaze target (3); animation head = gesture blend weights (8, softmax) + gait parameters (5). All decode from one 16-dim latent. Training loss is the equal-weight sum of embedding reconstruction and the four per-group variance-normalised MSEs.
- Phase 2 targets are synthetic: seeded random loadings per categorical field, one intent x social interaction, tanh squashing, plus noise 0.05. The corpus has no real behaviour or animation ground truth, so the gate tests whether the latent degrades gracefully under truncation. It does not test that real behaviour decodes from text.
- Phase 2 gate criteria were fixed before running: monotone within 1e-3 at every k from 16 to 4, and no single step above 35% of the total rise. Not retuned after the 3-seed result; the 10-seed rerun used the same criteria plus a pre-set accept rule (see DONE).
- Hub queue is a scripted service window (front agent released every 45 frames, queuers rejoin at the tail). Corridor door is a hard clamp on a 1.6 m gap at x=30.

- Phase 3 cost model: `frame_ms = theta_core * N + sum theta[axis,tier<3] * n[axis,tier]`. Tier 3 of each axis is the zero-cost reference so the fit is identifiable; `theta_core` absorbs the invariant-core fixed cost plus lowest-tier costs. RLS recursion is unclamped (clamping inside the recursion diverged); costs read out are clamped >= 0. P-trace cap at 1e4 per feature guards against blow-up under forgetting.
- Allocator: rows sorted (cost asc, quality desc) so argmax tie-break prefers the cheaper row. lambda_lo = 0, lambda_hi = max U / min positive cost gap (every agent at min cost). Stop when hi-lo <= 1e-4 hi or budget slack <= 1e-3 budget, max 64 steps. Returned assignment is always the evaluated feasible end `hi`, then greedy fill by utility-gain/cost ratio while upgrades fit. If min-cost assignment exceeds the budget, return it with `infeasible=True`; never violate the error headroom mask.
- Config quality q(c) = unweighted mean of four per-axis fidelities in [0,1]. Error rate e(c) = behaviour nMSE + navigation deviation (visual axes contribute zero divergence). Couplings: impostor geometry excludes IK animation; field/core navigation excludes gesture-bearing behaviour (latent16, latent8). Dominance pruning is a runtime helper (`prune_dominated`), not applied by default.
- Oracle: brute force validates the DP at N=6 (5-row subtables); the DP is the N=200 oracle. Tests quantise costs to integer ticks and hand the same quantised costs to both solvers, so the comparison is exact.

- Phase 4 tie-break: parallel versions scan the unsorted table with an explicit comparator (score desc, cost asc, quality desc, index asc); this equals first-argmax over the serial's lexsorted rows, so the per-frame sort is gone and the fixed table can sit in constant memory.
- FMA contraction breaks bit-identity of score = (s*q) - (lam*c). NVVM contracts by default and numba exposes no flag, so real-hardware CUDA uses libdevice `dmul_rn`/`dadd_rn` (never contracted); the simulator uses plain ops. C builds use `-ffp-contract=off` (clang) / `-Mnofma` (nvc). Numba CPU does not contract without fastmath.
- Aggregate cost: threaded sums `c[a]` with numpy in agent order, the oracle's own order, so it matches for float costs too. CUDA sums per-block partials in fixed order (deterministic run to run) but not in numpy's order; with integer-tick costs the sum is exact, with float costs a last-bit difference could flip a knife-edge bisection decision. Acceptance is on ticks plus float instances that happened to agree.
- Numba binds `cuda.const.array_like` at compile time, so only quality and err (fixed) go in constant memory; the per-frame RLS cost vector is staged into shared memory by each block (1.4 KB). A CUDA-C port would `cudaMemcpyToSymbol` it. Kernels are compiled per table via a closure factory.
- CUDA host loop: launch 8 kernels, read the done flag (one 64 B D2H), repeat; early-exit launches cost a few us each. A cooperative-groups persistent kernel would remove even that but cannot run under the simulator, so it was not written.
- Occupancy statement for `k_eval` on T4 (sm_75, 40 SMs, 64 KB shared/SM, 64K regs/SM, 32 warps/SM, 16 blocks/SM, 64 KB constant): TPB 256 = 8 warps; static shared 4.1 KB (eval) / 6.1 KB (fill) per block, so shared allows 15 blocks/SM; constant 4 KB of 64 KB; threads allow 4 blocks/SM. Registers decide: <= 64 regs/thread gives 4 blocks = 32/32 warps (100%), 65-80 regs gives 3 blocks (75%). `occupancy_report(alloc)` prints regs/thread and the driver's active-blocks figure on hardware. fp64 runs at 1/32 rate on Turing: the inner loop is 2 mul + 1 sub + compare per (agent, row), ~54M fp64 ops at N=100k, of order 0.2 ms per evaluation, so per-launch overhead and the ~15 launches per frame matter more than ALU.
- Greedy fill is inherently sequential (one global argmax per step). Each step is a full N x m pass in every implementation; at N=10k the benchmark instance took 18 fill steps, more than the 13 Lagrangian evaluations. Kept for oracle identity; a capped or parallel-batch fill is a phase 5/8 decision.
- Threaded uses numba `prange` rather than Python threads; `threads=k` sets `numba.set_num_threads`. Numba could not analyse a reduction on a variable assigned in the inner loop, hence the array write plus host sum.
- `tests/conftest.py` and `alloc/cuda/__init__.py` set `NUMBA_ENABLE_CUDASIM=1` when no `nvidia-smi` is on PATH (or on macOS). On the T4 leave it unset. `numba` was installed (phase spec names Numba-CUDA); no other dependency added.

- Phase 5 WEE without Nsight: the kernel's control flow is a deterministic function of its inputs, so the counters Nsight would read (thread_inst_executed / inst_executed) are reproduced two ways: the instrumented kernel writes per-lane neighbour-loop trip counts and per-warp (max trips, sum trips, class-body mask, tier-body mask) from shared memory; the host computes the same from the sort order and the grid (Kofler et al. cost model). Static instruction weights per region (`W_FIXED/W_GATHER/W_ENTRY/W_TRIP`) turn counters into issued and executed instructions. A warp issues the gather loop max(trips) times and every distinct body its lanes need. The test asserts the counters agree; the sweep raises if they differ on hardware.
- Phase 5 bytes moved is a sector model (distinct 32 B sectors per warp across own rows, cell ranges, cell lists, gathered pos/vel), i.e. L1-miss traffic to L2 assuming a warp's working set stays in L1. The crowd fits in the T4's 4 MB L2, so DRAM traffic is not what ordering changes. Achieved bandwidth = modelled bytes / kernel time.
- Phase 5 modelled time (simulator only, labelled `source=model`): max(issued / (40 SM x 4 SMSP x 0.5 IPC x 1.59 GHz), sectors x 32 B / 1 TB/s) + 4 us launch. It is memory-bound at sparse/mixed and issue-bound at dense, which is what makes the best w switch. Both constants are assumptions; the T4 replaces the whole column with CUDA-event medians and the policy recalibrates from the CSV.
- Phase 5 key family: the first version tried (`[coarse Morton][state][agent id]`) makes w=0 spatially blind; there w=0 was dominated at dense and w=1 nowhere, and the front was monotone (dense: w=0.4 418 B / 0.720, w=0.5 309 / 0.714, w=1 197 / 0.688). Kept as the `blind` reference only; the three-level key is the one reported because Morton inside a state group is free and strictly better.
- Phase 5 densities are tiled: the N=2000 simulator snapshot is replicated k x k (agents shuffled so the id tie-break carries no spatial information). 2000 agents is 63 warps, far too few to occupy 40 SMs, so per-w differences at N=2000 were within launch overhead in the model and would be latency-bound on the T4.
- The tier field in the key is the previous frame's tier (invariant 5). The study takes it from the phase 1 threshold assigner because the allocator needs telemetry costs; the hook for the allocated condition is the `assign` readback in `CudaAllocator.allocate`, and the key is agnostic to the source.

- Phase 6 scoring definition: the phase 3 table quality is agent-independent, so a GEMM needs a per-agent utility. The retained-energy profile phi_i = z_i^2 / |z_i|^2 makes behaviour and animation quality the fraction of the agent's own latent energy the tier keeps (linear in phi, hence a GEMM); nav and geo stay table constants (kappa_c). For a flat profile the structure matches the table, but the values differ (energy share k/d vs 1 - nMSE). Whether the allocator adopts the per-agent utility is a phase 7/8 decision; phase 6 compares INT8 against FP64 on the same utility.
- Phase 6 deviations from the confirmed derivation, all refinements of the same expansion: (1) the constant feature moved out of the GEMM into the epilogue, because a constant 1 set every row scale to 1/127 while energy shares are well below 1 (provable fraction at d = 16, lambda = 8.7 went from 0.19 to 0.54 at N = 300); the fp32 rounding count rises to 10u and the fp64 reference term to 5 2^-53. (2) Column scale max/126 instead of max/127 so the basis entries {0, 1/4, 1/2} quantise exactly (fbar_c = 0), and measured per-row residual caps ebar_i instead of Delta_i/2. (3) A pairwise interval on the score difference against the INT8 winner, eps_i(c, c*) = ebar_i |b_c - b_c*|_1 (+ the fbar terms, zero here), which is zero for rows sharing the winner's behaviour and animation tiers; it is the default and lifts the provable fraction from 0.36-0.89 to 0.94-0.99 at lambda > 0. The per-candidate interval remains available (`pairwise=False`) and is reported alongside.
- Phase 6 GEMM backend: Numba-CUDA has no WMMA/mma intrinsic, so the tensor-core path is `torch._int_mm` (cuBLASLt IMMA on sm_75), with K padded to 32 and m to a multiple of 8. Torch was already a dependency. The NumPy INT32 matmul is the oracle; on the T4 `gemm_bitexact` in the CSV must be True for every row since both are exact integer arithmetic.
- Reference and re-rank both use one njit sequential-order fp64 dot product, so the re-rank reproduces the reference's rounding exactly; a BLAS matmul could differ in the last bit and flip a knife-edge tie.

- Phase 7 KL measure: KL(cond || ref) at the fine (64-region) level, evaluated at realised states with fitted kernels, summed per agent since its last reconciliation (parity) or since t = 0 (baseline). At tier 3 the surrogate's fine-level kernel is the **sticky lift** (hold r while R is unchanged, draw r' ~ pi_ref(r' | R', ctx) on a region change); the memoryless lift proposed before implementation resampled the fine state every frame and charged the one-shot information loss H(r | R) every frame (3 nats/frame), so it was replaced. Frozen agents are charged -log P_ref(r | r), ticked-every-k agents KL(P_ref^k || P_ref) on tick frames and the frozen term on hold frames.
- Phase 7 per-frame rates are comparable between the surrogate and a frozen agent (walking: 0.010-0.011 vs 0.011 nats/frame; near_goal/chokepoint 0.05-0.06 vs 0.02). At hazard 1/90 the reference barely moves per frame, so "stay" is not surprising; the surrogate pays for region changes it makes. What separates the conditions in (a) is the forced restoration, not a lower rate; what separates them in state law is the region occupancy, which the surrogate matches by construction (MH) and a frozen agent does not.
- Phase 7 ledger: accrues the measured per-context surrogate rate e_rate[ctx] (agent-dependent), while the table's behaviour-tier-3 error rate is the worst rate over contexts with frequency > 1%, so the feasibility mask is conservative. Tiers 0-2 have rate 0 (they run the reference process; truncated decode is a quality loss, not a state divergence). Navigation error is zeroed in the phase 7 table because navigation tiers do not execute; the phase 3 placeholder remains in `alloc/config.py` untouched. Measured KL exceeds the ledger by up to 1.8x at the max because the ledger uses the context mean while the measured increment is per (ctx, region); p95 sits at the cap.
- Phase 7 budget: only the behaviour axis executes, so only its measured cost (plus the core) enters the budget; the non-executing axes cost 0 in this experiment (with the phase 3 stand-in geometry cost of 250 us the 6 us behaviour saving would never be chosen). Budget is expressed as floor + fraction of the allocatable range.
- Phase 7 baseline tick semantics (resolves the earlier open question): MassLOD sim tiers HIGH/MED/LOW tick the process and motion every 1/3/10 frames with accumulated dt (k process steps per tick, so the tick-time law is P^k); OFF is frozen. No extrapolation. No core, arrivals from fine position.
- Phase 7 core owns egress exactly: arrival is declared from s >= L, respawn positions and exits come from the world rng in completion order, and nothing in the core reads fine state, so under parity egress is byte-identical across camera paths (the tolerance in (b) is only a ceiling on how far the visible position can lag: BAND). The core's chokepoint capacity kappa (corridor door, 0.215 agents/frame) and the context-mean speed scale mbar come from a free full-fidelity calibration run without the core; the reference condition is full fidelity **with** the core.
- Phase 7 corridor route passes through the door centre (30, 5) so the core never crosses the wall; the chokepoint arc-length is taken at x = 29.5 on the first segment.

- Phase 8 conditions: `reference`, `baseline`, `parity`, `parity_nocap` (invariant 3 ablation, cap = 1e9 nats), `parity_authored` (invariant 2 ablation: quality = hand weights (0.4, 0.2, 0.2, 0.2) on authored ramps (1, 0.75, 0.5, 0); costs and rates unchanged). Grid counts 100/200/500/1000/2000/5000, `--frames 3000` below 2000 agents and `--frames-big 600` at or above. Calibration reused from the phase 7 N=200 cache per scene (surrogate kernels, kappa and per-agent cost are N-independent by construction; the sweep does not recalibrate per N).
- Phase 8 port numerics: fp32 `precise` scores plus integer-tick costs; the port's oracle is a Burst fp32 job with the same statement order, not the Python fp64 oracle. Fill capped at 8 dispatches, evaluations at 40 with `ForceFinish` returning the feasible bracket end. Ledger stays on the CPU; headroom sent to the GPU carries a margin of `L_max * e_max` for the readback latency.
- Phase 8 figures: Python frame times are prototype numbers; figure 3 picks its target from {33.3, 100, 333} ms for the prototype and uses 16.7 ms only for the engine CSV with the same schema.

- Unity demo compares at **matched spend** by default, because the user asked for an apples-to-apples comparison and a free-running budget is not one: MassLOD has no budget, so "25% of the tier span" held PARITY to an arbitrary number. Each policy's spend is priced by its own measured costs (MassLOD's tiers are frame strides, PARITY's are latent widths), and geometry by the one renderer both use.
- Geometry cost in the engine is wall time with a GPU sync, not CPU submit time alone: CPU-only would have missed the fill and shadow cost that makes the impostor tier cheap or not (in one launch it measured *more* expensive than tier 2, because alpha test disables early-z).

- View salience is projected **area**, (d0/d)^2, not height: the quality it multiplies is a count of wrong pixels measured at d0, and pixel count falls as 1/d^2. d0 = 10 m is also where the judge stands.
- The Python sim keeps the single-salience allocator. Its view axes cost nothing in phase 7 (only behaviour is timed), so a view salience would change no decision there, and switching would only perturb recorded timings.

- LLM tier runs on a local model through Ollama's HTTP API with the standard library: no Python dependency, nothing leaves the machine. Two model weights were pulled into Ollama (qwen2.5:1.5b, 986 MB; qwen2.5:7b, 4.7 GB).
- The scheduler never uses a past reply AS a decision (a response cache). With 576 discrete contexts a cache would trivially solve the problem, and it would not transfer: real LLM agents carry memory and dialogue, so contexts rarely repeat exactly. It does use past replies to know drift exactly at a context it has seen -- see OPEN QUESTIONS for what that becomes when contexts are continuous.

## OPEN QUESTIONS
- PARITY's default view machinery (coverage salience + pop ledger + switching cost + smoothing) loses 20-40% per-frame image fidelity to knapsack_fullsim. Its benefit is temporal (bounded pops), which no engine metric measures yet. Keep it default only if a pop metric shows the trade is worth it; otherwise area salience + free switching is the main path and pop bounding an option.
- Is the coverage buffer's rectangle occluder too aggressive against real silhouettes in a raised-camera crowd? `parity_free` (coverage) vs `parity_area_free` needs a matched-frame-time comparison.
- Phase 8, **settled and worse than predicted**: `parity_authored` was expected to coincide with `parity` in KL but to separate in tier mix. It does **not** separate anywhere. Across all 114 cells the two conditions differ only in frame-timing fields; every KL, ledger, restoration, band, egress and tier field is byte-identical. Cause: tier mix under every parity condition is exactly `0.25 / 0 / 0 / 0.75` and tiers 1-2 are selected in **zero** parity cells (they appear only in 9 baseline cells, via the MassLOD bands). Tiers 0-2 cost within 0.2 us and carry rate 0, so tier 0 dominates 1-2 on quality at equal cost and the allocator's choice is binary, live-or-surrogate. Reweighting the quality column cannot change a decision with no alternatives. **The invariant-2 ablation therefore produces no signal at all and cannot support a claim either way as configured.** Options: (i) drop it, and say in the caption that invariant 2 is not separable in this telemetry; (ii) give tiers 1/2 real measured decode work at k = 8 / 4, recalibrate, re-run the three parity conditions. Figure 1 currently draws it dashed so it does not hide the parity line.
- Phase 8, **figure 3 is not publishable from this sweep**: `ft_low1_ms` (the 1% low, the number `figures/SPEC.md` says to quote) is dominated by machine noise, not by the system. `parity` and `parity_authored` make byte-identical tier decisions yet their 1% lows differ by up to 84% (plaza N=500: 14.56 vs 89.65 ms; corridor N=2000: 755.8 vs 519.4). The sweep ran for hours on a contended laptop, and the slowest-1% statistic picks up OS scheduling, not allocator behaviour. `ft_mean_ms` is far steadier (same pair within 6% at most counts). Either re-run the timing cells on a quiet machine, or quote the mean for the prototype figure and reserve the 1% low for the engine CSV where it means something.
- Phase 8: at T* = 33.3 ms the `reference` condition sustains **more** agents than `parity` in plaza (923 vs 775) and corridor (987 vs 380); parity wins only in hub (891 vs 845). The spec's check warns rather than fails here, correctly: the serial allocator is 11-29% of the parity frame at N=5000 (corridor 0.286) while the behaviour axis only saves ~6 us/agent, and the frame is dominated by O(N^2) separation that no condition changes. As it stands figure 3 does not support "PARITY sustains more agents"; that claim needs either the threaded/GPU allocator, real per-tier costs on the other three axes, or a cheaper neighbour structure.

- Phase 6, **settled on hardware, and the tensor-core framing does not survive it**. INT8 IMMA over fp32 is only **1.15-1.47x** — 1.27-1.37x at N=2000 and 1.41-1.47x at N=50000. Raising N from 2000 to 50000 barely moved it, so the earlier "needs N >= 50k to mean anything" explanation was wrong: the operation is not launch-bound, it is **output-bandwidth-bound**. At N=50k, m=184 the fp32 output alone is 50000 x 184 x 4 B = 36.8 MB, about 115 us at the T4's ~320 GB/s against a measured 274 us, while the arithmetic is only ~0.59 GFLOP against a 130 TOPS INT8 peak (~1.6% of peak). INT8 shrinks the inputs, not the output, so it cannot buy much here. Correctness is untouched and strong (bit-exact, 0.94-0.99 provable); the honest claim is "INT8 scoring is exact and certifiable", not "tensor cores make scoring fast".
- INT8 row resolution is set by the dominant first latent dim. A per-row two-scale split (head dim separately) or INT8 on log-energy would tighten the intervals; not done, since the pairwise interval already certifies 94-99% at lambda > 0.
- Phase 5, **settled on hardware, and it reverses the modelled conclusion**. Measured CUDA-event time per density at N=50k (us): sparse w=0 **513.7** vs w=1 845.0; mixed w=0 **940.8** vs w=1 1077.2 (blind 937.0, a 0.4% tie inside noise); dense w=0.1 **2453.5**, w=0 2565.0, w=1 2723.1. The model had predicted w=1 best at sparse/mixed and w=0 at dense; measurement says w=0 or near-0 everywhere. So the modelled "best w" column in phase 5 DONE is wrong and must not be quoted.
  - The phase 5 time model is off by roughly **100x in absolute terms** (dense w=0: 2565 us measured vs 16.98 us modelled; sparse 513.7 vs 12.58). Both of its constants were assumptions and at least one is badly wrong — 1 TB/s is A100-class, the T4 has ~320 GB/s. Bytes and WEE, which are counted rather than assumed, survive intact; only the time column dies.
  - Pareto criterion (a) is arguably now **met** in measured time: on the held-out sweep the best fixed w is 0.3 (mixed 16k), 0.2 (mixed/sparse 32k) and 0.1 (sparse 16k, dense 32k), so intermediate w beats *both* extremes at several points and neither extreme is universally optimal. This is the outcome the criterion was written for, and it only appears with real timings.
  - Adaptive criterion (b) now **FAILS** on held-out data: 5 of 6 density/size groups are worse than the hindsight-best fixed w, by 4-18% (`python -m order.adaptive`). It passes on calibration trivially. Cause: the policy's features are log1p(rho) and geff with no N term, and it only ever selects from {0.0, 0.1} because those won at the N=50k calibration size; but the optimum moves to 0.2-0.3 at 16k-32k, where the grid does not saturate the SMs. Fix is to add an occupancy/N feature and recalibrate across sizes, not just densities.
